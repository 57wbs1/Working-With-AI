Chapter four. What the toolkit costs.

There are seven tools in this chapter. You need about two of them.

Everyone converged on twenty dollars a month, because that happens to be the number people pay without thinking about it. The free tiers have become genuinely good, which complicates the picture in a useful way.

Let me go through what each one is for.

ChatGPT is the broadest. Biggest feature set, biggest ecosystem, and a free tier that now carries ads.

Claude handles long documents and careful reasoning, and Claude Code comes included from twenty dollars. If you want to build things, that is the cheapest serious setup available.

Perplexity is an answer engine. It searches the live web, reads what it finds, and puts a citation on every claim so you can check it. If you are a student, Education Pro is ten dollars instead of twenty, verified with an academic email, and almost nobody claims it.

Gemini lives inside Gmail and Docs and Drive, and copes well with very long inputs.

Gemini Notebook, which was called NotebookLM until July, only answers from sources you upload. That restriction is the whole feature. It cannot wander into half-remembered training data, because it has nothing else to draw on.

Gamma builds a first-draft deck from a prompt in about two minutes.

And Higgsfield generates video and images. The presenter clip at the start of each chapter came from there.

Now, the part worth remembering. Nothing is unlimited any more. Both of the two-hundred-dollar tiers are metered. Claude Max is the same model with more headroom, so if you are not hitting limits you are buying nothing at all.

Prices moved four times last quarter. Check them before you quote me on any of this.
