Chapter five. Why prompting is dead.

There was a whole industry built around prompt engineering. Magic phrases, elaborate templates, telling the model it was a world-class expert.

All of that was a workaround for models that could not follow ordinary instructions. They can now. That skill has quietly expired.

What replaced it is a different job entirely.

You stop writing steps, and you start stating the end state. The constraints it has to work inside. And how you will judge whether it worked. Then it finds its own route, which is frequently a route you would not have chosen.

Three things go into every delegation.

The end state. What is true when this is finished? Something like, I can walk into the Thursday tutorial able to defend a position on this.

The constraints. Word count, which sources it may use, the house format, what it must never do. Those mark the bounds.

And the test. How will you both know it worked? A worked example. A check it has to pass.

You already know how to do this, by the way. It is mission command. Intent, constraints, freedom of action, back-brief. The only new part is that this particular subordinate will never tell you when it is out of its depth.

Which brings us to skills. A skill is a folder holding written instructions. It sits there doing nothing until a task matches its description, and then the model loads it and follows it.

That is how you stop repeating yourself. Your standards go in once, and they apply every time after that.

And the fastest way to write one is to skip writing it. Do a real job with the model, correct it as you go, and then say, turn everything you just learnt into a skill. It writes the file for you. Then cut half of what it produced, because it will over-generalise.
